# 15-Week E-Commerce Course Outline (Computing) — Africa & Global Digital Markets Edition

## Course Information

**Course Title:** E-Commerce — Mobile Money Systems and Cryptocurrency
**Duration:** 15 Weeks
**Credits:** 4
**Prerequisites:** Basic programming knowledge, Introduction to Web Development, Database Fundamentals
**Course Code:** BCSEC 6204

**Unit Focus:** This unit treats mobile money and cryptocurrency not as a side topic but as a core thread running through the course. Since most digital commerce in East Africa is transacted through mobile money rather than cards, and since cryptocurrency and blockchain-based payment rails are an increasingly live option for cross-border trade and remittances, students need fluency in both to operate credibly in the region's digital economy.

---

## Course Aim

This course equips students with a well-rounded grasp of electronic commerce — its systems, enabling technologies, and strategic dimensions — from a computing perspective. Learners will build both the technical craft and the business judgment needed to design, build, launch, and run e-commerce solutions, while staying alert to the legal, security, and ethical rules that shape online trade. A deliberate emphasis is placed on how these global concepts play out in African markets, particularly East Africa, where mobile-first infrastructure, mobile money, informal trade digitization, and regional trade integration (such as the African Continental Free Trade Area) are reshaping how digital commerce actually works on the ground. Graduates will leave able to connect technical build-out with business strategy anywhere in the world, and with specific fluency in adapting global e-commerce models to African market realities.

---

## Course Goals

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. **Explain** the core building blocks of e-commerce — what it is, how it emerged, and the business models that run on it — including models adapted to African informal and formal trade
2. **Break down** the technology stack that powers e-commerce platforms, from infrastructure to application layer
3. **Build** working e-commerce solutions using current web technologies and platforms, including low-bandwidth and mobile-optimized approaches suited to East African connectivity conditions
4. **Assess** security risks in e-commerce and put in place suitable protections for transactions
5. **Apply** the legal and regulatory rules that govern e-commerce, both domestically (including Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania, and Rwanda's data protection and consumer laws) and internationally
6. **Set up** online payment systems and understand how financial transactions are processed, with particular fluency in mobile money ecosystems (M-Pesa, MTN Mobile Money, Airtel Money) and a working grasp of cryptocurrency, stablecoins, and blockchain-based payment rails, alongside card and digital wallet options
7. **Use** analytics and decision-support tools to shape e-commerce strategy in markets with variable data availability
8. **Build** skills in supply chain management and customer relationship management, including last-mile delivery and logistics solutions for markets with informal addressing systems and rural reach challenges
9. **Craft** digital marketing strategies suited to online audiences, including mobile-first and social-commerce approaches common across East Africa
10. **Practice** leadership, ethical conduct, and professionalism in e-commerce settings, including inclusive design for underbanked and unconnected populations

## 15-Week Course Outline

**Topics:**
- What e-commerce is: definitions, scope, and why it matters
- How e-commerce evolved globally and its trajectory across Africa
- E-commerce versus traditional commerce: what changes and what's gained
- Business models: B2C, B2B, C2C, C2B, G2C — and how informal/social commerce (WhatsApp selling, Instagram shops, boda-boda delivery networks) fits alongside them
- Current trends and outlook, including Africa's leapfrog to mobile-first digital trade

**Learning Activities:**
- Lecture and discussion on e-commerce definitions and evolution
- Case study analysis: Amazon and Alibaba compared against Jumia and Kilimall
- Group discussion on how e-commerce is reshaping both global and African traditional retail (markets, dukas, kiosks)

**Readings:**
- Chapter 1: Introduction to Electronic Commerce
- Case studies on successful global and African e-commerce ventures (Jumia, Twiga Foods, Wasoko)

- Technical architecture documentation

**Lab Exercise:**

### Week 5: Mobile Money Systems in E-Commerce

**Topics:**
- Payment gateway fundamentals and how mobile money fits into the wider payments landscape
- Card processing and PCI compliance, as a point of comparison to mobile-money-first models
- How mobile money actually works: SIM-based wallets, USSD menus, agent networks, and cash-in/cash-out mechanics
- The major East African mobile money systems: M-Pesa (Safaricom), MTN Mobile Money, Airtel Money, and how each exposes APIs for merchant integration
- Interoperability between mobile money providers and between mobile money and banks
- Contactless and mobile payments more broadly

**Learning Activities:**
- Workshop on integrating a payment gateway with a mobile money option
- Case study: comparing PayPal and Stripe integration with Flutterwave, Pesapal, and the Safaricom Daraja (M-Pesa) API
- Discussion on how mobile money has driven financial inclusion for populations without traditional bank accounts

**Readings:**
- Payment system security guidelines
- PCI DSS compliance requirements
- Mobile money API documentation (Safaricom Daraja, MTN MoMo API)

**Lab Exercise:**
- Integrate a mobile money sandbox (e.g., Daraja) into a demo e-commerce checkout flow

---

### Week 5B: Cryptocurrency and Blockchain in E-Commerce

**Topics:**
- Core concepts: what a blockchain is, how cryptocurrency transactions are validated, and why this matters for commerce
- Cryptocurrency as a payment method: wallets, exchanges, and merchant acceptance
- Stablecoins and their growing use for cross-border trade and remittances into and out of East Africa, where currency volatility and high remittance fees are a real constraint
- Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs) and pilot programs on the continent (e.g., Nigeria's eNaira) as a government-led alternative to private cryptocurrency
- Regulatory stance on cryptocurrency across East African jurisdictions — largely cautious or unregulated, in contrast to the more mature regulatory treatment of mobile money
- Risks: volatility, fraud, consumer protection gaps, and money-laundering concerns that shape how (and whether) merchants adopt crypto payments
- Where blockchain shows real promise beyond payments: supply chain provenance tracking and trade documentation

**Learning Activities:**
- Workshop on setting up a cryptocurrency payment option in a sandbox environment
- Case study: stablecoin-based remittance flows into East Africa compared with traditional remittance channels
- Debate: should a small e-commerce merchant in the region accept cryptocurrency payments today, given the regulatory and volatility risks?

**Readings:**
- Introductory blockchain and cryptocurrency literature
- Central bank statements and guidance on cryptocurrency in Kenya, Uganda, and Tanzania
- Case material on stablecoin remittance corridors and African CBDC pilots

**Lab Exercise:**
- Configure a cryptocurrency payment sandbox and compare the transaction flow, fees, and settlement time against the mobile money integration built in Week 5

## Applied Focus: E-Commerce in the Digital World and African Industries

This section highlights themes that should be reinforced throughout the course, beyond what any single week covers, to ensure students can translate concepts into real-world African e-commerce practice.

### Mobile-First and Mobile Money Infrastructure
Most East African internet users access the web primarily or exclusively through mobile devices, and mobile money (M-Pesa in Kenya and Tanzania, MTN Mobile Money and Airtel Money across the region) is the default payment rail rather than cards. Students should be able to design and build for this reality rather than treating it as an edge case.

### Cryptocurrency and Blockchain as an Emerging Rail
Alongside mobile money, cryptocurrency and stablecoins are gaining real traction for cross-border payments and remittances, where currency volatility and high transfer fees make traditional channels costly. Students should understand both the opportunity (faster, cheaper cross-border settlement) and the constraints (thin regulation, price volatility, consumer protection gaps, and uneven central bank positions across the region) well enough to advise a business on whether and how to adopt it.

### Informal Trade Digitization
A large share of commerce across the region happens through informal or semi-formal channels — market vendors, small dukas, WhatsApp- and Facebook-based selling. Digitizing these channels (digital ledgers, social commerce storefronts, USSD ordering) is a distinct design problem from building a conventional online store.

### Logistics and Last-Mile Delivery
Formal addressing systems are often absent, and delivery relies heavily on motorcycle couriers, agent pickup points, and hub-based distribution. Students should understand how logistics providers (e.g., Sendy) and marketplaces solve for this.

### Regional and Continental Trade Integration
The East African Community Common Market and the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) are reshaping the rules for cross-border digital trade, tariffs, and standards harmonization across the continent — directly relevant to any e-commerce business planning regional expansion.

### Financial Inclusion and Alternative Credit
With lower card and traditional banking penetration, e-commerce businesses in the region increasingly rely on mobile money, agent banking, and alternative credit scoring (using mobile and transaction data) to serve buy-now-pay-later and micro-credit customers.

### Regulatory Landscape
Students should be conversant with Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act, Kenya's Data Protection Act, Tanzania's Personal Data Protection Act, and similar East African frameworks, alongside global standards like GDPR, since many regional platforms must comply with both.

### Regional Case Studies to Draw On
- **Jumia** — pan-African marketplace and logistics model
- **Kilimall and Jiji** — regional marketplace competitors
- **Twiga Foods** — B2B agricultural produce distribution
- **Wasoko (formerly Sokowatch)** — B2B distribution for informal retailers
- **Sendy** — logistics-as-a-service for e-commerce
- **Safaricom M-Pesa / Daraja API** — mobile money payment infrastructure
- **Flutterwave and Pesapal** — regional payment gateway aggregators
- **Nigeria's eNaira** — an African central bank digital currency pilot, useful as a contrast to private cryptocurrency
- **Stablecoin remittance corridors** — emerging cheaper alternatives to traditional cross-border money transfer into East Africa

## Career Pathways

This course prepares graduates for diverse roles in the e-commerce industry, both globally and within Africa's fast-growing digital economy:

### E-commerce Developer
- Design and develop e-commerce platforms and applications, including for low-bandwidth environments
- Implement front-end and back-end solutions
- Integrate payment systems (including mobile money) and third-party services
- Ensure website security and performance optimization

### E-commerce Manager
- Oversee e-commerce operations and strategy
- Manage online store performance and user experience
- Coordinate cross-functional teams
- Drive revenue growth and customer acquisition, including in underpenetrated regional markets

### Digital Marketing Specialist
- Develop and execute digital and social-commerce marketing campaigns
- Manage SEO/SEM, social media, and messaging-app-based marketing (e.g., WhatsApp Business)
- Analyze marketing data and optimize conversion rates
- Create content strategies for online audiences

### E-commerce Product Manager
- Define product vision and roadmap for e-commerce features
- Conduct market research and competitive analysis, including regional competitor landscapes
- Prioritize features and manage product development
- Collaborate with engineering, design, and marketing teams

### E-commerce Business Analyst
- Analyze business processes and identify improvement opportunities
- Gather and document requirements for e-commerce projects
- Create data-driven insights and recommendations
- Bridge communication between technical and business stakeholders

### E-commerce Consultant
- Advise businesses on e-commerce strategy and implementation
- Conduct assessments and develop recommendations
- Guide digital transformation initiatives, including for traditional businesses moving online
- Provide expertise on platforms, technologies, and best practices suited to regional market conditions

### Fintech and Digital Payments Specialist *(regionally significant addition)*
- Design and implement mobile money and digital wallet integrations
- Work on alternative credit scoring and financial inclusion products
- Navigate regional payment regulation and interoperability standards
- Support cross-border payment solutions under regional trade frameworks

### Logistics and Fulfillment Technology Specialist *(regionally significant addition)*
- Design last-mile delivery and fulfillment systems for informal and rural markets
- Build and manage logistics tracking and routing technology
- Optimize hub-and-spoke and agent-based distribution networks
- Coordinate with courier and delivery partners

## Conclusion

This 15-week E-Commerce course gives students a solid grounding for thriving in a fast-moving digital marketplace, with deliberate attention to how e-commerce concepts translate into African and East African realities — mobile-first infrastructure, mobile money, informal trade digitization, last-mile logistics, and regional trade integration under frameworks like AfCFTA. The course leans on hands-on practice, ethical grounding, and continuous learning to prepare students for a career in the ever-evolving world of digital commerce.
